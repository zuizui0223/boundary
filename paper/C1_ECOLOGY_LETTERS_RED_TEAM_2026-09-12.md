# C1 Ecology Letters Perspective — editorial red-team

Date: 2026-09-12  
Decision: **GO if activated, subject to the human send gates in `C1_SEND_READINESS_2026-09-12.json`.**

This red-team asks whether the Boundary proposal could fail at the unsolicited-proposal stage even if its mathematics is correct.

## 1. Risk: it reads like a methods paper rather than a Perspective

**Failure mode:** `k-rank(M)`, row-span tests and proxy bounds could make the proposal look like a technical method awaiting a Method-paper route.

**Current defence:** the proposal's headline is not a new estimator or software package. It introduces an **identification axis for mechanistic evidence** and uses rank/proxy geometry to make that conceptual distinction operational.

**Keep:** lead with the ecological distinction—mechanistic proximity versus discrimination among process explanations—before any formula.

**Do not do:** expand the proposal into algorithmic implementation details.

## 2. Risk: it appears to be a review or statement of opinion

**Failure mode:** Ecology Letters explicitly rejects Perspectives that are primarily topic reviews or unsupported opinion pieces.

**Current defence:** Boundary provides exact structural results: residual dimension `k-rank(M)`, the outside-row-span iff rank-gain condition, product-preserving unidentified directions, sharp proxy identified sets and a breakdown factor.

**Keep:** the proposal should state that the perspective is **quantitative and diagnostic**, not merely terminological.

## 3. Risk: it is primarily a summary of the authors' own work

**Failure mode:** the journal explicitly warns against Perspectives mainly discussing the authors' own work.

**Current defence:** the proposed ecological problem is broader than Boundary itself: mechanistic evidence, partial identification, measurement design, pollination quantity/effectiveness and seed-dispersal quantity/quality.

**Required before full manuscript:** keep the external literature architecture central. Boundary results should instantiate the perspective, not constitute the whole literature universe.

## 4. Risk: general ecological relevance is too narrow

**Failure mode:** identification geometry could be read as a specialized statistics issue.

**Current defence:** the same error occurs whenever a net ecological outcome is a product or chain of biological components but measurements collapse multiple process states. Pollination and seed dispersal provide independent ecological architectures rather than two versions of the same example.

**Strengthening rule if invited:** add at least one additional ecological process family only if it genuinely has the same identification structure; do not add decorative examples.

## 5. Risk: C1 duplicates C2

**Failure mode:** editors could reasonably ask why the geometry claim is not simply one section of `More measurement is not more evidence`.

**Firewall:**

- **C1 owns the full identification-axis argument**: structural dimension, row-span criterion, anchor ladder, proxy transport, sharp sets and breakdown factor.
- **C2 owns the cross-failure synthesis**: same direction / wrong target / shared blind spot / too late.
- C2 may use only the lesson that more measurement in an existing direction need not add identification.

If that firewall cannot be maintained in the final texts, do **not** run both papers simultaneously.

## 6. Risk: the proposal lacks an author-qualification statement

**Failure mode:** Ecology Letters editorial guidance for Perspective/Synthesis proposals recommends describing the qualifications of the proposed author(s).

**Current state:** deliberately unresolved rather than invented. The email now has an explicit author-qualification placeholder.

**Required before send:** fill one concise factual sentence and recheck the complete proposal packet against the 300-word ceiling.

## 7. Risk: proposal approval is mistaken for manuscript acceptance

**Failure mode:** an encouraged proposal can still be editorially rejected later.

**Rule:** proposal approval is only permission to submit the full Perspective. It is not evidence that the journal has accepted the framing or results.

## 8. Risk: activation timing damages the stronger C2 route

**Failure mode:** sending C1 and C2 simultaneously without an explicit publication firewall can look like concept slicing.

**Current strategy:** C1 remains parked while C2 is the primary broad concept pitch. Activate C1 if C2 is declined/too broad, or activate both only when their ownership boundary is explicit to authors and editors.

## Decision

**GO if activated.** There is no missing scientific analysis that blocks a proposal. The remaining issues are strategic activation, final authorship/qualification, contact metadata and live-rule recheck.

The correct next move is **not** to write the full 7500-word Perspective now. The correct move, if C1 is activated, is to finalize the <=300-word proposal packet and send it through the verified proposal-first route.
