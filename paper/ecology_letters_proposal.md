# Ecology Letters Perspective proposal

## Proposed title

**Mechanistic evidence needs an identification axis: measurement boundaries in ecological chains**

## Proposal

Across ecology, *mechanistic* can mean either measuring closer to biological machinery or collecting evidence that distinguishes competing process explanations. We argue that these are different properties and that mechanistic evidence needs an explicit **identification axis**. A measurement can be biologically proximal, precisely estimated and strongly associated with an outcome while still leaving multiple mechanisms compatible with the observations. For positive log-linear observation systems, that ambiguity has a simple structural form: exact observations with matrix `M` leave residual dimension `k-rank(M)`, and a new scalar measurement reduces that ambiguity if and only if it adds row rank. The practical consequence is a measurement-design rule: when the problem is structural non-identification, more replication or precision along an existing observation direction cannot substitute for a measurement that cuts a previously unresolved direction. Ecological chains make this distinction concrete. Net pollination service can combine visitation rate and per-visit effectiveness; seed dispersal can combine quantity and quality. Direct component measurements act as anchors that progressively reduce the unresolved mechanism space. Proxy uncertainty and breakdown analysis extend the same identification-axis logic when direct anchors are unavailable. The Perspective therefore reframes “more mechanistic measurement” as a testable question: **which mechanism distinctions does the current observation map actually identify, and which new observation would reduce the remaining ambiguity?**
