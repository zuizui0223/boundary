# Ecology Letters Perspective proposal

## Proposed title

**Mechanistic evidence needs an identification axis: measurement boundaries in ecological chains**

## Proposal

Across ecology, *mechanistic* can refer both to measurements close to biological machinery and to evidence that distinguishes competing process explanations. We propose a Perspective arguing that these are distinct evidentiary properties and that mechanistic claims need an explicit **identification axis**. The central limitation is structural: different mechanism states can be collapsed by the same observation map even when a null is rejected, model fit is excellent or sampling precision is high. For positive channels written on the log scale, exact log-linear observations with matrix `M` leave residual structural dimension `k-rank(M)`; a new scalar measurement reduces that ambiguity if and only if it adds row rank. Thus duplicate, rescaled or more precise measurements along an existing observation direction can improve precision without adding identification information. The familiar positive-product result is a direct corollary: for `W=prod_j F_j`, net-only observation leaves `k-1` product-preserving degrees of freedom and `r` independent direct channel anchors leave `k-1-r`. In proxy comparisons `X_i=q_iF_i`, the symmetric transport family `1/Gamma <= q_1/q_0 <= Gamma` yields point, partial and non-identification regimes, a sharp joint set and a reference-invariant **breakdown factor**. Pollination supplies a rate-by-effectiveness example and seed dispersal an independent quantity-by-quality architecture. The Perspective therefore turns generic limitation statements into an explicit diagnosis of which mechanism distinctions the current observation map cannot resolve, without treating null testing, sampling uncertainty or counterfactual causal estimands as interchangeable with mechanism identification.
