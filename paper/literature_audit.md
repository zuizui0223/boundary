# Mechanistic-evidence literature audit

## Audit question

What can be claimed about biological proximity and identification strength without caricaturing molecular ecology, observational ecology or causal-inference work?

## Result

The literature supports two mechanistic aspirations: proximity to biological machinery and discrimination or characterisation of causal processes. It does **not** justify claiming that ecology formally endorses a universal one-dimensional hierarchy in which molecular data are intrinsically more mechanistic than field data.

### Ecological genomics

Ungerer, Johnson & Herman (2008) explicitly motivate ecological genomics by understanding genetic mechanisms in natural environments. Rudman et al. (2018) show how genomic data can deepen mechanistic understanding of eco-evolutionary change while also stressing that genomic data alone are not sufficient for the broad eco-evolutionary questions considered. These papers support the value of proximity, not an automatic identification guarantee.

### Causal mechanisms

Grace et al. (2025) distinguish causal-effect estimation from causal-mechanism investigation and emphasise that the latter has its own evidentiary requirements. The present paper adds a narrower identification question: conditional on declared alternatives and an observation map, are those alternatives distinguishable?

### Intermediate processes and design

Correia, Dee & Ferraro (2025) show that inference about intermediary ecological processes requires explicit definitions, assumptions and design. This independently supports treating missing links as design problems and making unverifiable assumptions visible through sensitivity analysis.

### Field-level mechanistic evidence

Smith et al. (2020) show how field experiments using camera-trap data can test mechanistic predator–prey drivers under natural conditions. The relevant difference in inferential strength is design and discriminatory structure, not a move to a molecular measurement level.

### Observational causal design

Siegel & Dee (2025) emphasise that observational ecological data can answer causal questions under explicit design and assumptions. The target differs from the latent-channel identification problem here, but the design-first logic is compatible.

## Licensed claims

- Molecular and genomic measurements can provide genuine mechanistic proximity and strong mechanistic information.
- Proximity alone does not guarantee that a unique competing mechanism has been distinguished.
- Field measurements can test mechanisms when their design separates alternatives.
- Intermediate-process inference depends on observation design and assumptions.
- Evidentiary status is conditional on the scientific target, candidate mechanism set and observation map.

## Claims to avoid

- “Ecology believes molecular data are mechanism and field data are pattern.”
- “Molecular data are not mechanistic.”
- “Genomics cannot identify mechanisms.”
- “Field observations are generally more mechanistic than molecular data.”
- “Biological measurement level and identification strength are statistically independent.”
- “All mechanistic evidence reduces to point identification.”

## Terminology

Use **distinct dimensions**, **non-equivalent properties**, and **no monotone relation is assumed**. Avoid *orthogonal* as the primary adjective because it may imply statistical or geometric independence.
