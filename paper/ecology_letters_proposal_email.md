**Mechanistic evidence needs an identification axis: measurement boundaries in ecological chains**

Across ecology, *mechanistic* can refer both to measurements close to biological machinery and to evidence that distinguishes among competing process explanations. We propose a Perspective arguing that these are distinct evidentiary properties and that mechanistic claims need an explicit **identification axis**. The central limitation is structural: different mechanism states can be collapsed by the same observation map even when model fit is excellent or sampling precision is high. For positive channels written on the log scale, exact log-linear observations with matrix `M` leave residual structural dimension `k-rank(M)`; a new scalar measurement reduces that ambiguity if and only if it adds row rank. Thus more precise or repeated measurements along an existing observation direction can improve estimation without separating additional mechanisms. Product-structured ecological chains make the design consequence concrete: net outcomes can leave component trade-offs unresolved, whereas independent direct anchors progressively remove unidentified directions. Pollination rate × effectiveness and seed-dispersal quantity × quality provide two ecological architectures in which the next useful measurement is determined by the unresolved mechanism direction rather than by biological proximity alone. When direct anchors are unavailable, bounded proxy uncertainty provides a secondary extension from point to partial to non-identification, without changing the headline claim. The Perspective therefore turns “more mechanistic measurement” into a diagnostic question: **which competing mechanism distinction does the proposed observation actually resolve?**
