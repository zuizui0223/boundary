# Reviewer-objection audit

## 1. “You are redefining mechanism to mean identification.”
No. Mechanistic proximity, mechanistic modelling and mechanistic identification are related but distinct. The paper adds identification strength as an evidentiary dimension; it does not replace other meanings of mechanism.

## 2. “This is anti-genomics.”
No. Molecular and genomic measurements can be proximal, experimentally manipulable and highly identifying. The conditional claim is only that proximity alone does not guarantee discrimination among declared alternatives.

## 3. “The product non-identifiability is trivial.”
Concede the algebra. The contribution is the ecological evidence classification, the exact observation-rank criterion, the `k-1-r` coordinate-anchor corollary, the calibration-transport family, sharp joint set, breakdown factor and operational design/reporting consequences.

## 4. “The multiplicative model was chosen to manufacture the theorem.”
Pollination and seed dispersal independently use rate-by-effectiveness or quantity-by-quality products. The theorem audits an existing measurement architecture; it does not claim all ecology is multiplicative.

## 5. “Community service is a sum, not one product.”
Correct. `V_mE_m` is a within-type product. `sum_m V_mE_m` adds a separate across-type allocation ambiguity.

## 6. “The `k-1-r` rule assumes independent anchors.”
Yes. Redundant measurements do not reduce dimension twice. State `r independent direct channel anchors` every time. The more general exact statement is `dim(C_y)=k-rank(M)` for the declared log-linear observation matrix.

## 7. “Channel and calibration anchors are being mixed.”
They solve different missing-information problems. Channel anchors reduce latent-stage equivalence dimension. Calibration anchors measure proxy conversion and cross-regime transport.

## 8. “The complete ecological chain is not necessarily multiplicative.”
Agreed. The theorem applies only after a positive multiplicative endpoint map is scientifically declared. Other maps require their own identifiability analysis.

## 9. “Relative proxy ratios cancel calibration.”
Only under transport stability, `q_1/q_0=1`. Absolute scale cancels; regime-specific conversion drift does not.

## 10. “Why believe Gamma?”
Do not infer it from the same W/X observations. Use external calibration, validation, instrument/design knowledge or a declared sensitivity grid. The breakdown factor reports the minimum distortion sufficient to overturn the conclusion.

## 11. “F and E intervals can be reported independently.”
No. The same `kappa` generates both ratios and every admissible pair satisfies `rho_F rho_E=rho_W`. The primary object is the joint set.

## 12. “The 34% claim depends on reference direction.”
Use `Gamma*=max(rho_hat,1/rho_hat)` or `eta*=|log rho_hat|` as the canonical robustness scale. “34% upward drift” is only a directional translation.

## 13. “Isn’t this just null-hypothesis testing?”
No. A null test can reject one null statement while several non-null mechanisms remain observationally equivalent. The paper asks whether the current observation map separates declared mechanism alternatives. `reject H0` is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for a unique mechanism decomposition.

## 14. “Isn’t this just residual error or poor fit?”
No. Competing mechanisms can fit the observed target exactly and still be structurally non-identifiable. The paper's `residual structural dimension` means dimensions left free by the observation constraints; it is not a regression residual or unexplained variance.

## 15. “Wouldn’t more samples or more precise measurements solve this?”
They can reduce sampling uncertainty, which is valuable, but they do not reduce structural nullity if they leave the observation row span unchanged. In the exact log-linear class, duplicate, rescaled and more precise versions of existing observation directions add no identification rank. Do not generalise this into the false claim that more data never help.

## 16. “Isn’t this already counterfactual causal inference?”
No. Identification of an intervention effect and identification of a full mechanism decomposition are different inferential targets. A causal contrast can be point identified while several biological mechanism decompositions remain compatible; conversely, two mechanisms can sometimes be discriminated by an observational measurement when they make different observable predictions. The paper complements causal inference and does not treat observational discrimination as a substitute for intervention when the estimand itself is causal.

## Submission stop conditions

Do not submit if molecular and field evidence are intrinsically ranked; `orthogonal` is used to imply independence; `Gamma` is described as estimated from the same net/proxy data; N4 is described as a `delta->1` endpoint; channel and calibration anchors are conflated; `k-1-r` omits independence; sum-of-products is presented as one two-factor product; elementary algebra itself is sold as the primary novelty; null rejection is treated as mechanism identification; regression residuals are confused with structural residual dimension; repeated sampling is claimed to cure unchanged observation-map invariance; or observational discrimination is presented as a replacement for counterfactual causal identification.
